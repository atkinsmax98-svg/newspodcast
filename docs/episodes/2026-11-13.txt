In our physiology segment, Marathon Handbook examines a growing trend in sports nutrition: oral lactate supplements. The article investigates whether consuming lactate products can genuinely boost endurance capacity or delay exhaustion. While lactate was long mischaracterized as a metabolic waste product responsible for muscle fatigue, modern sports science recognizes it as a vital fuel source for working muscles and cardiac tissue during intense exercise. Marathon Handbook reviews current scientific evidence, concluding that while theoretical benefits exist, evidence proving clear performance gains from commercial lactate supplements remains limited, and traditional aerobic training remains the most effective way to improve lactate clearance and utilization.

If you have ever traveled to high altitude for a race or vacation and felt instantly sluggish, Outside Magazine offers some reassuring physiological perspective in an article titled, "It's OK, You're Supposed to Be Slow at Altitude." The piece explains that because atmospheric pressure drops at higher elevations, fewer oxygen molecules enter the bloodstream with every breath, leading to an immediate increase in heart rate and breathing effort even at easy paces. Outside Magazine reminds athletes to adjust pace expectations downward when training above sea level and to allow two to three weeks for natural physiological adaptations, such as increased red blood cell production, to take effect.
