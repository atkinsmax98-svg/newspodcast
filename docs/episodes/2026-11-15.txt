Beyond race results and trail reports, sports scientists and medical researchers are constantly gathering new data on human movement, exercise physiology, and risk management.

A study published by Bioengineer.org provides intriguing new insights into running mechanics and energy consumption. Researchers examined how stride rate impacts metabolic cost at the gas exchange threshold—the point during incremental exercise where ventilation begins to increase rapidly relative to oxygen consumption. The study revealed that running at a lower cadence, meaning taking fewer and longer strides per minute at a set speed, significantly increases a runner's metabolic cost. In practical terms, maintaining a slightly higher, shorter stride cadence helps distance runners conserve vital energy and delay fatigue when running near their aerobic threshold.
